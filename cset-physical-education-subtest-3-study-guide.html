1. What Is CSET Physical Education Subtest III (131)?

CSET Physical Education Subtest III (Test Code 131) is the third and final subtest required to demonstrate subject matter competency for a Single Subject Teaching Credential in Physical Education in California. Like the other PE subtests, it's administered by Pearson on behalf of the California Commission on Teacher Credentialing (CTC).

Where Subtest I covers the science of movement and Subtest II covers applied movement forms and assessment, Subtest III is the most conceptual and reflective of the three. It asks you to step back from any single skill or sport and think about physical education as a profession and a field of study: its history and philosophy, the legal and ethical responsibilities of a PE teacher, and how to apply movement science thoughtfully to real students with real differences.

Subtest III is computer-based only, administered at a Pearson test center, and — like Subtest I — is not available through online proctoring. You must pass all three subtests (129, 130, 131) to complete the CSET Physical Education subject matter requirement through examination.

📖 New to this cluster? Start with the CSET Physical Education Subtest I (129) Study Guide or see the Subtest II (130) Study Guide for movement concepts and assessment.

2. Exam Structure

Subtest III consists of 40 multiple-choice questions and 1 extended constructed-response question, administered over 1 hour and 45 minutes. It's the only PE subtest with just one constructed-response item — but that item carries more weight and requires a longer, more developed response than the short/focused items on Subtest I and II.

Question TypeNumberFormat
Multiple Choice 40 questions Split across Professional Foundations and Integration of Concepts
Constructed Response 1 question (extended) Built around a video excerpt; a written response of roughly 150–300 words, designed to take about 30–45 minutes

Scoring is weighted 70% multiple-choice and 30% constructed-response, the same proportion used across all three PE subtests. Because there's only one constructed-response prompt, it's worth treating that single response with real care — there's no second attempt within the section to balance out a weak answer.

Subtest III is offered as a computer-based test at an in-person Pearson test center only. Candidates can take it on its own or combine it with Subtests I and II in a single test session.

Registration & Fees

  • $99 to take Subtest III on its own
  • $297 to take all three subtests (129, 130, 131) together
  • Testing is offered by appointment during scheduled testing windows throughout the year
  • Check ctcexams.nesinc.com for the current registration calendar and results release dates

3. Topics Covered

Subtest III is built from two content domains. Integration of Concepts carries more weight than Professional Foundations, but both require genuine understanding rather than memorized facts — this subtest rewards candidates who can connect ideas, not just recall them.

Domain 1 — Professional Foundations

This domain tests your understanding of physical education as a field: its history, guiding philosophies, legal and ethical responsibilities, and professional infrastructure.

Topic AreaWhat You Need to Know
Philosophies of Physical Education Past and present philosophies of PE and how they've shaped the goals and structure of contemporary programs
Historical Development The history of physical education, including contributions from educators across different backgrounds and eras
Current Research, Trends, and Issues Contemporary issues affecting PE — such as inclusion, lifelong fitness, and youth health trends — and their impact on programs
Legal and Ethical Issues Supervision, liability, confidentiality, equity, disability accommodation, and diversity considerations in PE settings
Interrelationships of the Subdisciplines of Kinesiology How areas like biomechanics, exercise physiology, motor behavior, and sport psychology connect and inform one another
Professional Responsibilities, Organizations, and Resources Professional organizations that support PE (such as national associations), and current state and national PE standards
Relationship Between Human Movement Activities and Values How movement activities reflect or challenge traditional values, and the role of PE in promoting equity for diverse groups

Domain 2 — Integration of Concepts

This domain tests your ability to apply subject matter knowledge to real students — adapting activities, sequencing skill development, and connecting PE to the rest of a student's education.

Topic AreaWhat You Need to Know
Interpretation and Application of the Subdisciplines of Kinesiology Applying kinesiology knowledge (biomechanics, motor learning, physiology, etc.) to support student skill acquisition and performance
Selecting, Adapting, and Modifying Activities Using developmental information to adjust sports and games for different skill levels, individual needs, and disabilities
Developmental Progressions Appropriate sequencing of skills within and between movement categories as students develop
Learning Concepts and Principles Applying principles of learning to understand and respond to individual differences among students
Connections Between Physical Education and Other Disciplines Meaningful links between PE and subjects such as science, social science, health, math, language arts, and the arts

Important: Integration of Concepts asks you to combine knowledge from earlier subtests — motor development, biomechanics, movement forms, assessment — and apply it to specific student scenarios. If your Subtest I and II content feels rusty, a quick review before starting Subtest III will make this domain much easier.

4. How Difficult Is the Exam?

Difficulty depends heavily on how much formal exposure you've had to the philosophical, historical, and legal side of the profession — not just the practical or scientific side. Here is an honest breakdown:

BackgroundLikely ExperienceTypical Prep Focus
Recent kinesiology or PE credential coursework Professional Foundations content is likely familiar from required courses; mainly a refresher Practice applying concepts to scenarios for Integration of Concepts, and rehearse the extended response
Experienced PE teacher, coursework was years ago Strong intuitive sense of adapting activities; historical, legal, and philosophical details may be rusty Refresh Professional Foundations terminology and current standards/organizations
Coach or athletic background, limited formal PE training Comfortable adapting activities practically, but legal/ethical issues and PE history are often new Dedicate focused time to Professional Foundations before moving to Integration of Concepts
Career changer, strong general teaching background Comfortable with learning principles and differentiation concepts; PE-specific history and kinesiology connections are likely new Build PE-specific content in both domains, leaning on general teaching experience for the Integration of Concepts scenarios

Candidates most often underestimate Subtest III because it doesn't test a specific skill or sport — it tests how well you can reason about the profession as a whole. That makes it harder to cram and more dependent on genuinely understanding the material.

5. Recommended Study Timeline

This 5-week plan works for most candidates, especially those who have already worked through Subtest I and II content. Extend it if Professional Foundations topics are entirely new to you.

Week 1

Diagnostic + Philosophy & History

Take a diagnostic practice set to identify weak areas. Study philosophies of physical education and the historical development of the field.

Week 2

Trends, Legal & Ethical Issues

Cover current research, trends, and issues in PE, then move into legal and ethical topics: supervision, liability, confidentiality, equity, and disability.

Week 3

Kinesiology Connections & Professional Landscape

Review how the subdisciplines of kinesiology relate to one another, then study professional organizations, standards, and the relationship between movement activities and values.

Week 4

Integration of Concepts

Work through applying kinesiology knowledge to real scenarios, adapting activities for different needs, developmental progressions, and connections to other subjects.

Week 5

Extended Response Practice + Full Review

Practice the extended constructed response using video-based prompts, aiming for a 150–300 word response within 30–45 minutes. Finish with a full timed run-through (1 hour 45 minutes) and review any missed concepts.

6. Best Study Strategies

📜
Build a timeline of PE's history and philosophies
A simple timeline connecting major philosophies and historical shifts to their impact on today's programs makes recall far easier under exam pressure.
⚖️
Study legal and ethical issues as real scenarios
Don't just memorize terms like liability or confidentiality — practice recognizing them in short classroom scenarios, since that's how they're often tested.
🔗
Practice connecting PE to other subjects
Integration of Concepts rewards candidates who can name specific, concrete links to science, math, health, and the arts — not vague generalities.
🎥
Rehearse the extended, video-based response
Since there's only one constructed-response item, practice this format specifically — watch a clip, plan quickly, and write a complete 150–300 word response.

For the constructed-response question specifically

  • Spend the first few minutes outlining your response after watching the video — this is an extended item, so structure matters more than on the short items in Subtest I and II
  • Aim for a clear introduction, supporting points, and conclusion within the 150–300 word range
  • Reference specific subject-matter concepts from Professional Foundations or Integration of Concepts, not general opinions
  • Leave a few minutes at the end to reread and tighten your response before submitting

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common pitfalls candidates run into

  • Treating Professional Foundations as "soft" content — history, philosophy, and legal/ethical topics are tested as specifically as any other domain
  • Underpreparing for the single extended response — because there's only one, it's tempting to under-practice; that's a mistake given how much weight it carries
  • Giving vague answers on Integration of Concepts — the domain rewards specific, applied reasoning over general statements
  • Confusing scaled score with percentage — 220 out of 300 is not 73%; don't set a percentage target
  • Skipping review of Subtest I and II content — Integration of Concepts leans on kinesiology and movement knowledge from the earlier subtests
  • Running out of time on the extended response — without a rough outline first, it's easy to lose track of structure within the 30–45 minute window

9. Frequently Asked Questions

What does CSET Physical Education Subtest III (131) cover?
Subtest III covers two domains: Professional Foundations (philosophy, history, legal and ethical issues, and the subdisciplines of kinesiology) and Integration of Concepts (adapting activities, developmental progressions, learning principles, and connections between physical education and other subjects).
How many questions are on CSET PE Subtest III?
Subtest III has 40 multiple-choice questions and 1 extended constructed-response question. The session is 1 hour and 45 minutes and is computer-based only.
What is the constructed-response question like on Subtest III?
Subtest III includes a single extended constructed-response question built around a short video excerpt. You watch the clip, then write a response of roughly 150 to 300 words, typically designed to be completed in about 30 to 45 minutes.
Is CSET PE Subtest III hard?
Difficulty depends on your background. Candidates with strong classroom teaching experience often find Integration of Concepts intuitive, while Professional Foundations content — history, philosophy, legal and ethical issues, and professional organizations — is frequently unfamiliar unless it was covered directly in coursework.
How is Subtest III different from Subtest I and II?
Subtest I tests the science of motor development and human movement, and Subtest II tests applied movement forms and assessment. Subtest III is more conceptual — it covers the history, philosophy, and legal/ethical foundations of the profession, plus your ability to integrate and apply concepts from the other domains to real student scenarios. It also has just one constructed-response question instead of two, but that question is extended rather than short.
Should I take all three PE subtests together or separately?
Either is possible — you can register for one subtest, two, or all three in a single test session. Many candidates take Subtest III last, since Integration of Concepts draws on material from Subtest I and II.
