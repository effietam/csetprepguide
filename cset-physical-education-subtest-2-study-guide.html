1. What Is CSET Physical Education Subtest II (130)?

CSET Physical Education Subtest II (Test Code 130) is the second of three subtests required to demonstrate subject matter competency for a Single Subject Teaching Credential in Physical Education in California. Like the other PE subtests, it's administered by Pearson on behalf of the California Commission on Teacher Credentialing (CTC).

Where Subtest I is built around the science of movement and motor development, Subtest II shifts toward the practical and applied side of physical education: the psychological and social dimensions of movement, the full range of physical activities a PE teacher is expected to teach, and how to assess and evaluate student performance fairly and accurately.

Subtest II is also structurally different from Subtest I and III in one important way: it's the only one of the three subtests you can take through online proctoring, in addition to the standard computer-based test at a Pearson center. You still need to pass all three subtests (129, 130, 131) to complete the CSET Physical Education subject matter requirement through examination.

📖 Already working through Subtest I? See the CSET Physical Education Subtest I (129) Study Guide for the growth, motor development, and human movement science domains.

2. Exam Structure

Subtest II consists of 40 multiple-choice questions and 2 short (focused) constructed-response questions, administered over 1 hour and 30 minutes. It's shorter than Subtest I and III, but covers three domains instead of two.

Question TypeNumberFormat
Multiple Choice 40 questions Split across three domains, weighted toward Movement Concepts and Forms
Constructed Response 2 questions (short/focused) Typed directly into the testing interface; no video component

As with the other PE subtests, scoring is weighted 70% multiple-choice and 30% constructed-response. Unlike Subtest I, Subtest II does not include a videotaped constructed-response component — both constructed-response items are text-based prompts.

You can take Subtest II as a standard computer-based test at a Pearson test center, or remotely through online proctoring from a location of your choice. If you test online, be aware that breaks are not permitted during the session, while restroom breaks are allowed (and count against your time) if you test at a physical center.

Registration & Fees

  • $99 to take Subtest II on its own, whether at a test center or via online proctoring
  • $297 to take all three subtests (129, 130, 131) together at a test center
  • Computer-based testing is offered during scheduled testing windows throughout the year; online proctoring windows run on a separate, shorter schedule
  • Check ctcexams.nesinc.com for the current registration calendar and results release dates

3. Topics Covered

Subtest II is built from three content domains of different sizes. Movement Concepts and Forms carries far more weight than the other two combined, which should shape how you divide your study time.

Domain 1 — The Sociology and Psychology of Human Movement

This domain tests how movement and physical activity connect to personal growth, motivation, and social development in students.

Topic AreaWhat You Need to Know
Personal Development How physical activity contributes to self-esteem, self-discipline, and individual growth
Theories Related to Motivation Motivation theories relevant to physical activity, including intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation and social learning theory
Social Development How movement activities build cooperation, communication, leadership, and conflict-resolution skills
Role of Movement Activities in Society The broader social, cultural, and historical role physical activity and sport play in communities
Factors Influencing Activity Choices Age, gender, culture, access, and other factors that shape which activities students engage in

Domain 2 — Movement Concepts and Forms

This is the largest domain on Subtest II. It tests your practical knowledge across the full range of movement forms a PE teacher is expected to instruct.

Topic AreaWhat You Need to Know
Fundamental and Creative Movement Skills Locomotor, non-locomotor, and manipulative skills, and creative movement exploration
Dance Concepts and Forms Rhythmic movement, folk and social dance forms, and choreography basics
Gymnastic Movements Balance, rolling, weight transfer, and other core gymnastics skills and progressions
Aquatic Skills Fundamental swimming and water-safety skills and how to teach them
Individual, Dual, and Team Sports and Games Rules, skills, and tactics across a range of sports and games taught in PE settings
Outdoor Education Activities and skills associated with outdoor and adventure-based education
Nontraditional and Cooperative Activities Cooperative games and nontraditional activities (such as pickleball or bocce) that build teamwork
Combative Activities Basic combative and self-defense activities and the skills and safety considerations involved
Fitness Activities Structured fitness activities such as weight training and aerobic conditioning

Domain 3 — Assessment and Evaluation Principles

This domain tests how you measure and interpret student progress and performance in physical education.

Topic AreaWhat You Need to Know
Evaluation Methods in Physical Education Formative and summative evaluation approaches used in a PE setting
Techniques of Test Construction, Evaluation, and Administration How to build and administer a fair, valid assessment of physical or motor skills
Test Characteristics Core testing concepts such as validity, reliability, and objectivity
Assessment Techniques and Tools Practical tools used to assess psychomotor, cognitive, and social-emotional outcomes in PE
Types of Evaluation Norm-referenced vs. criterion-referenced evaluation and when each is appropriate
Basic Statistical Applications Foundational statistics used to interpret assessment results, such as mean and percentile
Interpretation and Communication of Assessment Data How to interpret results accurately and communicate them to students, families, and colleagues

Important: Movement Concepts and Forms makes up more of the multiple-choice section than the other two domains combined, so it deserves the largest share of your study time — but don't skip Assessment and Evaluation Principles just because it's the smallest domain; those questions are often more procedural and memorizable with focused review.

4. How Difficult Is the Exam?

Difficulty depends heavily on the breadth of your practical PE background. Here is an honest breakdown:

BackgroundLikely ExperienceTypical Prep Focus
PE teacher or student teacher Broad exposure to most activity areas; assessment principles and sociology/psychology theory may need direct review Fill gaps in less-taught activity areas (dance, aquatics, combatives) and review theory vocabulary
Coach or single-sport specialist Deep knowledge in one or two sports, but likely gaps across dance, gymnastics, aquatics, or outdoor education Systematically cover every activity category in Movement Concepts and Forms, not just your specialty
Kinesiology graduate, limited teaching experience Strong theoretical foundation; practical activity-specific knowledge may be thinner Focus on the applied, activity-based content in Movement Concepts and Forms
Career changer, general classroom background Comfortable with assessment concepts from general teaching; movement-specific content is likely new Build foundational knowledge across all three domains, starting with Movement Concepts and Forms

Candidates most often struggle when they assume their strength in one activity area (like a sport they played competitively) covers the breadth the exam actually requires. Movement Concepts and Forms spans nine distinct activity categories — reviewing all of them matters more than mastering one.

5. Recommended Study Timeline

This 6-week plan reflects how the exam is weighted, with the most time devoted to Movement Concepts and Forms. Adjust the pace based on how many activity areas are already familiar to you.

Week 1

Diagnostic + Sociology & Psychology

Take a diagnostic practice set to identify weak areas. Study personal and social development theory, motivation theories, and factors influencing activity choices.

Week 2

Movement Concepts, Part 1

Cover fundamental and creative movement skills, dance concepts and forms, and gymnastic movements.

Week 3

Movement Concepts, Part 2

Cover aquatic skills, individual/dual/team sports and games, and outdoor education activities.

Week 4

Movement Concepts, Part 3 + Assessment Basics

Finish Movement Concepts and Forms with nontraditional/cooperative, combative, and fitness activities. Begin Assessment and Evaluation Principles: evaluation methods and test characteristics.

Week 5

Assessment & Constructed-Response Practice

Finish Assessment and Evaluation Principles, including basic statistical applications. Write one short constructed response each day, alternating domains.

Week 6

Full Review + Timed Practice

Do a full timed run-through under exam conditions (1 hour 30 minutes). Review every missed concept, and spend your final days on your weakest activity category.

6. Best Study Strategies

🗂️
Build a checklist of all nine activity categories
Movement Concepts and Forms is broad. Track your confidence in each category (dance, gymnastics, aquatics, etc.) so nothing gets skipped.
🧠
Learn motivation theories by name
Sociology and psychology questions often reference specific theories directly — know the names and core ideas, not just the general concept.
📊
Get comfortable with basic assessment statistics
Mean, median, percentile, validity, and reliability show up in Assessment and Evaluation Principles — a short, focused review goes a long way.
💻
Decide: test center or online proctoring
If you're considering online proctoring, review the setup and no-breaks policy well before test day so it's not a surprise.

For constructed-response questions specifically

  • Keep responses short and focused — lead with your key point and support it with specific, relevant detail
  • Use accurate subject-matter terminology from the domain the prompt is drawn from
  • Where relevant, ground your answer in a realistic classroom or activity scenario
  • Leave a minute or two to reread your response before moving on

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common pitfalls candidates run into

  • Over-relying on one activity area — deep knowledge of a single sport doesn't cover the other eight categories in Movement Concepts and Forms
  • Skipping Assessment and Evaluation Principles — it's the smallest domain, but still worth focused review since it's often unfamiliar territory
  • Treating sociology/psychology as common sense — the exam expects you to know specific theories and terminology, not just general intuition
  • Confusing scaled score with percentage — 220 out of 300 is not 73%; don't set a percentage target
  • Not deciding early between a test center and online proctoring — each has different rules around breaks and environment that are worth planning around
  • Writing constructed responses that are too general — vague answers score lower than responses that use precise terminology and concrete detail

9. Frequently Asked Questions

What does CSET Physical Education Subtest II (130) cover?
Subtest II covers three domains: The Sociology and Psychology of Human Movement, Movement Concepts and Forms, and Assessment and Evaluation Principles. Movement Concepts and Forms is the largest domain, covering fundamental movement skills, dance, gymnastics, aquatics, sports and games, outdoor education, and fitness activities.
How many questions are on CSET PE Subtest II?
Subtest II has 40 multiple-choice questions and 2 short constructed-response questions across its three domains. The session is 1 hour and 30 minutes.
Can I take CSET PE Subtest II online?
Yes. Subtest II is the only CSET Physical Education subtest available through online proctoring, so you can test remotely, including from home. It's also available as a computer-based test at a Pearson test center.
Is CSET PE Subtest II hard?
Difficulty depends on your background. Candidates with broad practical PE teaching or coaching experience across multiple activity areas usually find Movement Concepts and Forms manageable, while Assessment and Evaluation Principles and the psychology/sociology theory in the first domain often need more direct study.
How is Subtest II different from Subtest I?
Subtest I focuses on the science of motor development and human movement — more academic and physiology-based. Subtest II is more applied: it covers the psychology and sociology of movement, the practical activities PE teachers instruct, and how to assess student performance. Subtest II is also shorter and is the only one available via online proctoring.
Do I need to take all three PE subtests at once?
No. You can register for one subtest, two, or all three in a single test session at a test center — but note that online proctoring is only available for Subtest II on its own, not combined with the other subtests.
